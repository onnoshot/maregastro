Fine Dining Etiquette: A Guide for First-Timers

November 16, 20268 min readMare Gastro

Short answer: Fine dining etiquette is simpler than it looks: arrive on time for your reservation, put your napkin on your lap, use cutlery from the outside in, judge a wine at the tasting only on whether it is faulty, and mention any allergy up front. The rest is simply being relaxed; in a good restaurant the service team will guide you anyway.

What Is Fine Dining, and Why Does It Have Its Own Etiquette?

Fine dining is a top-tier restaurant experience that brings together carefully chosen ingredients, refined presentation and attentive service. The point of the rules is not show but keeping the flow intact: the kitchen prepares plates in a set order, and the service team sets the table to match. We explained the concept itself in our post on what fine dining is.

The 12 rules below are drawn from points common to international guides and table-manners courses; next to each one we have also written how it works at Mare Gastro.

Before You Sit Down: Reservation, Time and Dress

  1. Make a reservation and mention any special circumstances. If details such as a birthday, an anniversary or an allergy are passed on in advance, the kitchen and service prepare accordingly. At Mare Gastro you can leave a note in the reservation form.
  2. Arrive on time, and let them know if you will be late. Calling or writing about a delay of more than 15 minutes makes it easier to hold your table.
  3. Dress to suit the venue. Some restaurants ask for a jacket; Mare Gastro has no strict dress code, and a relaxed-smart choice is enough.

At the Table: Napkin, Cutlery and Bread

  1. Put the napkin on your lap as soon as you sit. If you leave the table briefly, put it on the seat of your chair; when the meal is over, fold it and leave it to the left of the plate.
  2. Use cutlery from the outside in. Forks, knives and spoons are laid out from the outside in according to the order of service; knives and spoons go to the right of the plate and forks to the left (Ankara University open course, in Turkish).
  3. Signal when you pause and when you finish. Leaving the fork and knife crossed on the plate means "I'm still going"; leaving them side by side, parallel, means "I'm finished".
  4. Tear bread, don't cut it. Bread is broken into small pieces by hand; with mezes that are spread on bread, hold the bread in your left hand and the knife in your right.

What Should You Keep in Mind When Ordering?

  • Don't hesitate to ask questions. Asking the server about an ingredient or cooking method you don't know is a natural part of fine dining.
  • Specify how you want it cooked. For tenderloin and chops you will be asked for the doneness; if you're unsure, ask for a recommendation.
  • Ask about the portion. If the menu doesn't list weights, find out whether the plate is suitable for sharing.
  • Keep change requests reasonable. Changes for allergies take priority; rebuilding a plate from scratch strains the kitchen's flow.

How Do You Choose and Taste Wine?

  1. Tasting is a check, not a test of whether you like it. The server shows you the bottle and pours a small amount. If you smell no cork taint or vinegar when you sniff it, give your approval; not liking the wine is not a reason to send it back.
  2. Hold the glass by the stem. That way the wine doesn't warm up in your hand.

If you are unsure about a choice, tell the server what you've ordered and ask for a recommendation. Mare Gastro's list has 32 wines, 4 champagnes and sparkling wines, 31 rakı and 12 alcoholic cocktails (November 2026); details are in our post on alcohol-serving restaurants.

Talking to the Service Team: Allergies, Photos and Phones

  1. Mention allergies and diet before ordering. Information such as gluten, shellfish or dairy sensitivity needs to be known from the start so the kitchen can adjust the plate.
  2. Silence your phone and take photos without flash. Photographing plates is normal now; flash and long photo sessions disturb the other tables.
  3. Tipping is not mandatory but common. In Turkey, leaving a tip when you are happy with the service is customary; the amount is entirely up to you.

How Do You Ask for the Bill and Split It?

You ask for the bill by making eye contact with the server and giving a short signal; there is no need to call across the room. If you are in a group, saying before ordering that the bill will be split saves time at the end of the evening. For celebrations, the most elegant approach is to decide in advance who will pay, so it isn't debated at the table.

If You Are Celebrating a Special Day

On evenings such as birthdays, anniversaries or proposals, whether the surprises work depends entirely on the information passed on in advance. At Mare Gastro, cake, candles and table decoration are planned through the reservation note; cake is a separate item in the Extras section of the digital menu. If it is to be a surprise, make the reservation in your own name rather than the name of the person being celebrated. Guides by type of celebration: birthday, anniversary, marriage proposal.

6 Common Fine Dining Mistakes

  1. Mentioning an allergy after the plate has arrived. Had the kitchen known from the start, it could have changed the plate.
  2. Calling the server by waving or shouting. Eye contact and a light hand signal are enough.
  3. Saying "I don't like it" at the wine tasting and sending it back. The tasting is for finding out whether the wine is faulty.
  4. Dipping bread into the shared plate. Serve yourself from shared mezes onto your own plate with the serving spoon.
  5. Leaving the phone face up on the table. Silencing it and keeping it in a bag or pocket is more graceful.
  6. Debating the bill at length at the table. Who pays and how it is split: talk about it beforehand.

None of these is a serious fault; but when all of them are avoided, the evening runs more smoothly for you and for the service team.

What Should You Write in the Reservation Note?

  • The special occasion (birthday, anniversary, proposal) and whether it is a surprise
  • Allergy and diet information (gluten, shellfish, vegetarian)
  • Space preference: garden or indoor dining room
  • The chance of a delay, or a late arrival time

These details let the service set up the evening for you in advance. At Mare Gastro the note field is in the reservation form.

In what order is cutlery used?
From the outside in. Cutlery is laid out according to the order of service; for each course you start with the outermost fork and knife.
How do I show that I have finished my meal?
Leave the fork and knife on the plate side by side, parallel. Leaving them crossed shows that you are still eating.
What do you do at a wine tasting in a restaurant?
You smell and taste the small amount the server pours; the purpose is to check whether the wine is faulty. If there is no fault, you give your approval.
