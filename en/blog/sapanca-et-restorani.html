Lamb or Beef?

The two offer different experiences in the same evening. Beef tenderloin is a lean, tender cut with a neutral taste; it gains character from its sauce and sides. Lamb chops, with the fatty edge that cooks beside the bone and a pronounced aroma, leave a more "regional" taste. Slow-cooked or offal dishes such as the shank and kokoreç represent lamb in its most traditional forms.

Shank on a Bed of Keşkek: A Three-Hour Plate

The menu's most patient plate is the Shank Dream. The lamb shank cooks in a 180-degree oven for about three hours; it arrives on a bed of keşkek with the chef's own jus and mint oil. The keşkek tradition was inscribed on UNESCO's intangible cultural heritage list in 2011 (UNESCO); it is much loved in Sakarya and its surroundings too.

Cooking Levels: How Should You Ask?

When you order tenderloin or chops, the server asks for the doneness. The internal temperatures below are approximate values commonly used in kitchens; the meat warms a few more degrees while resting.

DonenessInsideApprox. internal temperature
RareRed in the center, cool50–52 °C
Medium rarePink-red in the center, warm55–57 °C
MediumPink in the center60–63 °C
Medium wellA slight trace of pink65–68 °C
Well doneNo pink70 °C and above

Lamb chops are usually preferred medium; for tenderloin, medium rare is the doneness that best keeps the juices. Pregnant guests are advised to choose well done.

Why Does Resting the Meat Matter?

If meat coming off the grill or pan is cut without resting for a few minutes, its juices run onto the plate and the meat dries out. During resting, the internal temperature rises a few more degrees and the juices redistribute inside the meat. That is why in a good kitchen meat is served after resting, not the moment it is cooked; the few minutes of waiting are part of the flavor.

Why Are the Sides So Important?

On a meat plate, the side is not decoration but balance. The creamy texture of the eggplant purée softens the fat of the lamb, keşkek blends with slow-cooked shank as if it came out of the same pot, and matchstick potatoes create contrast with their crunch. That is why every side on Mare Gastro's meat plates is chosen specifically for the plate.

For Offal Lovers: Liver Strips and Kokoreç

Offal is one of the most deeply rooted but least carefully treated sections of Turkish cuisine in restaurants. Mare Gastro has two offal dishes: 200 g Sliced Liver among the warm small plates and Lamb Kokoreç among the main courses. The liver is cooked quickly in thin strips so that it stays juicy; the kokoreç is balanced by the freshness of the shepherd's salad with pear and fresh lavash. Both are among the plates that pair best with rakı.

4 Questions to Ask When Ordering Meat

  1. How many grams is it? At Mare Gastro the tenderloin and the liver strips are listed on the menu as 200 g.
  2. Which doneness is recommended? If you are unsure, ask for the server's recommendation.
  3. Can the side be changed? If you have a diet or allergy, mention it when ordering.
  4. Which drink goes with it? Red wine or rakı; ask for a suggestion from the list.
