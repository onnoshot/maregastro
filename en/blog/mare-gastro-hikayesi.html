The Story of Mare Gastro: A Fine Dining Kitchen in Sapanca

Last updated: November 16, 20267 min readMare Gastro

Short answer: Mare Gastro is a fine dining restaurant that opened on 30 June 2026 in the garden of Didi Otel, right next to Lake Sapanca. Executive Chef Doğan Anapa leads the kitchen; the menu interprets Aegean and Mediterranean cuisine with the region's local products. Open 09:00–00:00 Tuesday to Sunday.

What Is Mare Gastro, and Where Is It?

Mare Gastro is a restaurant in the Kırkpınar Soğuksu neighborhood of Sapanca, in the garden of Didi Otel. The concept was designed by Kadir Çiçek within Didi Otel; the aim was to add a fine dining kitchen to this garden right next to the lake without disturbing the texture of nature.

Why "Mare"?

Mare means "sea" in Italian and Latin. The name describes the direction of the kitchen: the fresh, simple and ingredient-respecting approach of the Aegean and the Mediterranean. Sapanca is a lake town; yet the backbone of the menu is seafood, olive-oil dishes and seasonal vegetables.

That direction was clear from day one. In the first frames shared in May 2026, before the opening, there were green beans in olive oil, beetroot carpaccio, smoked eggplant and artichoke; underneath, a single sentence: "Mare, coming soon." (Instagram)

Who Is Behind the Kitchen?

Executive Chef Doğan Anapa is known for the sense of narrative he brought to the kitchen from his background in cinema; he builds every menu like a flow with a beginning and an end. He puts local produce at the center: Karasu asparagus, aged Thracian kaşar, Adapazarı's islama tradition and keşkek all find a place on the menu.

Fine Dining, but Unpretentious: The Philosophy of the Kitchen

Mare Gastro's approach is less about heavy, ceremonial fine dining and more about a simple presentation that puts the quality of the ingredient first. Little but right ingredients on the plate, a balanced sauce and a side that suits the season; the calm of the garden completes the rest.

That is why there is no dress code, the menu is à la carte and the table can be shared. Mezes are ordered for the middle, and the main course is chosen per person. Our post on Aegean and Mediterranean cuisine and our fine dining etiquette guide explain this approach in detail.
