What Is Aegean and Mediterranean Cuisine? Its Features and How They Reach the Table

November 16, 20268 min readMare Gastro

Short answer: Aegean cuisine is a regional Turkish cuisine that puts olive oil, wild greens, vegetables and seafood front and center, cooked in simple ways. Mediterranean cuisine is the shared table of a much wider basin, from Italy to Greece and from Spain to Morocco; UNESCO inscribed the "Mediterranean diet" on its intangible cultural heritage list in 2010.

What Are the Main Features of Aegean Cuisine?

Four elements define Aegean cuisine: olive oil, greens, seasonal vegetables and the sea. In the region, olive oil is used not only in vegetable dishes but in meat too; vegetables are often cooked by "sweating" them in olive oil rather than in water (yemek.com, in Turkish). Boiling, sautéing, baking and olive-oil cooking are preferred over frying.

  • Olive-oil dishes: artichoke, green beans, stuffed vine leaves, fava
  • Greens: chicory, nettle, radish greens, samphire, asparagus
  • Seafood: grilled fish, octopus, calamari, shrimp
  • Cheese and yogurt: Ezine cheese, haydari, cacık

What Is the Difference Between Mediterranean and Aegean Cuisine?

The two concepts overlap but are not the same thing. Aegean cuisine is a regional cuisine of Turkey's western coasts, while Mediterranean cuisine describes the shared table of many countries that share the same climate and vegetation. UNESCO inscribed the Mediterranean diet in 2010 on behalf of Spain, Greece, Italy and Morocco; Cyprus, Croatia and Portugal were added in 2013 (UNESCO).

Why Are Olive-Oil Dishes Eaten Cold?

Olive-oil vegetable dishes are traditionally served at room temperature or cold. As olive oil cools its flavor softens and the vegetable takes on the sauce; the dish tastes better a day later. That is why olive-oil dishes sit on the Aegean table before the main course as mezes and get along well with rakı or white wine.

What Do You Eat at an Aegean-Mediterranean Table?

A typical table opens with cold olive-oil dishes, continues with a plate of greens or vegetables and peaks with grilled fish or lamb. Dessert is usually light: seasonal fruit or a simple milk-based sweet.

Where Marmara Meets the Aegean: Sapanca's Products

Sapanca is not in the Aegean but in eastern Marmara; yet the products of Sakarya and its surroundings suit Aegean-Mediterranean cuisine very well. These local traces are clearly visible on Mare Gastro's menu:

  • Circassian cheese: From the region's Circassian culinary tradition; a mousse in the artichoke in olive oil, smoked on the cheese plate, the main ingredient in the cheesecake.
  • Hazelnut: On top of the hummus; Sakarya is one of the hazelnut-growing provinces.
  • Karasu asparagus: A local product that meets rock sea bass on the summer menu.
  • Islama and keşkek: Adapazarı's islama tradition is interpreted with calamari, and keşkek with slow-cooked lamb shank.

Is Aegean-Mediterranean Cuisine Healthy?

The Mediterranean style of eating, built on vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, fish and olive oil, is one of the patterns often recommended in nutrition guides. A restaurant evening is of course not a diet program; but a table built from olive-oil dishes, green salads and grilled fish ends up much lighter than a menu of heavy sauces.

5 Tips for Setting an Aegean Table at Home

  1. Start with a good extra virgin olive oil. Much of the flavor of an Aegean table comes from the olive oil.
  2. Sweat the vegetables in olive oil. Cooking over low heat in olive oil before adding any water preserves color and flavor.
  3. Add acid. Lemon, pomegranate molasses or vinegar lightens the plate.
  4. Leave the herbs for last. Dill, parsley and mint added just before serving keep their aroma.
  5. Prepare the mezes a day ahead. Olive-oil dishes get tastier as they rest.

What Do You Drink at an Aegean-Mediterranean Table?

The two classic companions of the Aegean table are rakı and white wine. Rakı goes with white cheese, melon and olive-oil mezes; white wine suits fish, seafood and plates with greens. Red wine comes to the fore mostly with lamb and baked dishes. 3 Common Misconceptions About Aegean and Mediterranean Cuisine

  • "Mediterranean cuisine is just salad." No; fish, lamb, legumes and grains are at the center of this cuisine too.
  • "Olive-oil dishes means oily." Olive oil is the cooking medium; a properly cooked olive-oil dish is light and fresh.
  • "Aegean cuisine can only be eaten in İzmir." Its technique and approach to ingredients can travel anywhere; at Mare Gastro in Sapanca this is done with local products.
